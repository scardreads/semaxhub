# Semax Regulatory Status

**Educational overview only. Not legal or medical advice.** Rules change by country and over time. This page explains the big picture in plain language so visitors can orient themselves; it is not a clearance to import, prescribe, compound, or use Semax.

---

## Short intro

Semax sits in an unusual regulatory spot: it is a **real registered medicine in Russia** (prescription nasal drops used in clinical practice there), yet it is **not approved** by major Western drug regulators such as the U.S. FDA or the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Understanding that split helps newcomers read headlines and product listings without mixing “approved drug in one country” with “legal for medical use everywhere.”

---

## In Russia: prescription nasal medicine

### What “registered” means here

In the Russian Federation, Semax is authorized as a medicinal product. Public manufacturer materials and prescribing texts cite registration numbers such as:

- **ЛС-002553** (dated 30 December 2011): Semax nasal drops 0.1%
- Related registrations also appear in manufacturer materials (e.g. **Р N000812/01**)

The pharmaceutical group on the label is a **nootropic** agent (ATC code **N06BX**: other psychostimulants and nootropics). The dosage forms discussed in Russian practice are **nasal drops**, historically including **0.1%** and a higher-concentration **1%** preparation developed for heavier cerebrovascular / stroke-oriented use.

### Prescription status

The official instruction states release from pharmacies **by prescription** (“По рецепту”). In plain language: in Russia it is treated as a **doctor-directed medicine**, not a casual grocery item: even though online chatter sometimes blurs that.

### Clinical-practice framing (Russian context)

Russian sources and reviews describe use in neurology and related fields (for example cerebrovascular cognitive problems, post-stroke recovery settings, optic-nerve disease in ophthalmology protocols, and other labeled indications). Those indications are **Russian regulatory and clinical-practice claims**. They are not FDA or EMA approved indications.

**Uncertainty flag:** Indication lists on manufacturer websites can sound broader than what a Western regulator would accept on the same evidence package.

## Vital and Essential Drugs list (ЖНВЛП): ~2011 context

### What the list is

Russia maintains a national **List of Vital and Essential Drugs** (Перечень жизненно необходимых и важнейших лекарственных препаратов, often abbreviated **ЖНВЛП** / ZhNVLP). Inclusion is about national health-system priority and price regulation for listed medicines: it is **not** the same thing as “proven by the FDA.”

### Semax and the ~2011 listing

Secondary references (including encyclopedic summaries citing WHO-archived Russian list materials) state that Semax appears on the Vital & Essential Drugs list associated with the Russian government’s order of **7 December 2011** approving the list for **2012** (Government Order **№ 2199-р**). Manufacturer materials also state that Semax is included among vital and essential medicines and in certain federal treatment standards.

**Plain-language meaning:** Russian authorities treated Semax as important enough for national essential-medicines policy around that period. That strengthens the “real drug in Russia” story. It does **not** create approval in the United States, EU, UK, Canada, Australia, etc.

**Uncertainty flag:** Essential-medicines lists are updated over years. Visitors checking current pharmacy or reimbursement status in Russia should verify the **current** ЖНВЛП / registry entry rather than assuming a 2011–2012 snapshot never changed.

### What Western non-approval does *not* prove

- It does **not** by itself prove the molecule is “fake” or that Russian clinicians never used it.
- It **does** mean Western regulators have not endorsed it for routine clinical care under their standards of evidence, manufacturing oversight, and labeling.

**Uncertainty flag:** Compounding, import, and research-chemical rules are jurisdiction-specific and change. This page does not map every country’s customs or pharmacy law.

## Who developed and manufactures it (regulatory identity)

Semax emerged from Russian peptide research associated with the **Institute of Molecular Genetics** (Russian Academy of Sciences) and collaborators (notably work connected with **N.F. Myasoedov** and **I.P. Ashmarin**). Commercial nasal products have been associated with **Peptogen** (Innovation Research and Production Center) in Moscow.

Knowing the named Russian medicine helps visitors distinguish **pharmacy Semax** from anonymous “Semax” powders or analogs marketed abroad.

## Practical orientation checklist for newcomers

1. **In Russia:** Registered prescription nasal drug; appears in essential-medicines / standards discourse; clinical literature exists in Russian neurology journals.
2. **In the U.S. / EU (and most Western markets):** Not an approved medicine for patient use the way prescriptions for licensed drugs work.
3. **Online “for research” labels:** Educational red flag for quality and legality: not a medical pathway.
4. **This site:** Explains status; does **not** sell, dose, or advise use.

---

## Uncertainty flags

- Registration numbers and list membership should be rechecked against current **ГРЛС** (state drug registry) and ЖНВЛП publications when absolute currency matters.
- CIS and neighboring-country status is **not uniform**; do not assume “approved in Russia ⇒ approved in all former Soviet states.”
- Advisory discussions in other countries (for example compounding-related committee chatter in secondary sources) are **not** the same as drug approval.
- Legal risk for individuals (import, possession, marketing claims) depends on local statutes: seek qualified local advice if needed.
