## Cornerstone papers (start here)

1. **Kolomin T, Shadrina M, Slominsky P, Limborska S, Myasoedov N.** A new generation of drugs: synthetic peptides based on natural regulatory peptides. *Neuroscience & Medicine*. 2013;4:223-252. [doi:10.4236/nm.2013.44035](https://doi.org/10.4236/nm.2013.44035)  
   IMG RAS–linked review: Semax/Selank design, Russian clinical framing, tolerability notes.

2. **Ashmarin IP, Nezavibat'ko VN, Myasoedov NF, et al.** [A nootropic adrenocorticotropin analog 4-10-semax (15 years experience in its design and study)]. *Zh Vyssh Nerv Deiat Im I P Pavlova*. 1997;47(2):420-430. [PMID: 9173745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9173745/)  
   Design memoir from the primary Russian literature.

3. **Dolotov OV, et al.** Semax, an analog of ACTH(4-10) with cognitive effects, regulates BDNF and trkB expression in the rat hippocampus. *Brain Research*. 2006;1117(1):54-60. [doi:10.1016/j.brainres.2006.07.108](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2006.07.108) · [PMID: 16996037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16996037/)  
   Strongest commonly cited mechanism paper (animal).

4. **Official Russian prescribing information.** Semax nasal drops 0.1%, registration **ЛС-002553** (30.12.2011); prescription release; ATC N06BX. [Package insert PDF](https://semax.ru/upload/iblock/867/867fc8166acadfb577dc956d855999a3.pdf).

## Origin and chemistry

- **Potaman VN, Alfeeva LY, Kamensky AA, Levitzkaya NG, Nezavibatko VN.** N-terminal degradation of ACTH(4-10) and its synthetic analog semax by the rat blood enzymes. *Biochem Biophys Res Commun*. 1991;176(2):741-746. [doi:10.1016/S0006-291X(05)80247-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-291X(05)80247-5) · [PMID: 1851003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1851003/)
- **Kaplan AY, Kochetova AG, Nezavibathko VN, Rjasina TV, Ashmarin IP.** Synthetic ACTH analogue Semax displays nootropic-like activity in humans. *Neurosci Res Commun*. 1996;19(2):115-123. (Early human/EEG-era report; small by modern standards.)
- Name etymology (“seven amino acids” / СЕМь АминоКиСлот): widely repeated secondary explanation, e.g. [Wikipedia: Semax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semax). Prefer primary chemistry papers above for the molecule itself.

**Folklore note:** Search nicknames like “KGB peptide / KGB brain spray” are not primary historical titles. Direct KGB operational origin is **not established** in the peer-reviewed trail we cite. See [Origin](/origin).

## Safety and product identity

- Official Russian label (ЛС-002553): mild nasal mucosal irritation with prolonged use; prescription dispensing. [Package insert PDF](https://semax.ru/upload/iblock/867/867fc8166acadfb577dc956d855999a3.pdf)
- Kolomin et al. 2013 (above): clinical tolerability notes in summarized experience (including nasal mucosa findings; limited diabetes/glucose note in one dataset)
- Manufacturer overview (promotional tone; use cautiously): [semax.ru: Что это](https://semax.ru/chto_eto/)

Visitor Q&A: [Safety & FAQs](/safety-faqs).

**Quality caveat:** Pharmacy Semax in Russia is not the same evidentiary or quality world as unregulated online “research” vials. We do not evaluate or recommend vendors.

## Gaps we still flag

- Large independent Western multicenter double-blind RCTs for many popular claims
- Full English texts for some Russian clinical series (abstracts only for many readers)
- Confirmed archival proof of a KGB “ops drug” origin (absent from our primary set)
- Head-to-head modern trials that cleanly separate Semax vs Selank for healthy-volunteer “nootropic” use

When we add a new claim on a teach page, it should land here too.
