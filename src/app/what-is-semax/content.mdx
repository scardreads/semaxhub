# What is Semax?

**One sentence:** Semax is a short synthetic peptide - seven amino acids long - developed in Russia as a longer-lasting relative of a natural brain-active fragment of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), and used there as a prescription nasal medicine for certain neurological contexts.

This page is a newcomer map. It tells you what Semax *is*, what it *is not*, and where to read deeper. **Educational only. Not medical advice.** Semax Hub does not sell Semax, recommend use, or provide dosing guidance.

---

## The molecule in plain words

Semax’s sequence is:

**Met–Glu–His–Phe–Pro–Gly–Pro** 
(single-letter code: **MEHFPGP**, Semax's seven-letter amino-acid code)

That makes it a **heptapeptide** (seven amino acids). The name **Semax** is widely explained as coming from the Russian idea of “**семь аминокислот**”: roughly “**seven amino acids**” (СЕМь + АминоКиСлот → СЕМАКС). Treat that etymology as the standard popular/secondary explanation; the chemistry itself is unambiguous.

Chemically, Semax is an **analog of ACTH(4–10)**: a short stretch of adrenocorticotropic hormone that older peptide research linked to central-nervous-system effects more than to adrenal steroid release. Designers kept a core related to that fragment and added a **Pro–Gly–Pro (PGP) tail** so the molecule would last longer against enzymes that normally chew peptides up in minutes. Peer-reviewed summaries describe the finished compound as **non-corticotropic**: selected to keep neurotropic interest while avoiding ACTH’s cortisol-raising hormonal activity.

> **What “peptide” means here**
>
> A peptide is a short chain of amino acids - the same building blocks as proteins, but much shorter. Semax is not a vitamin, not a stimulant like caffeine or amphetamine, and not the full ACTH hormone. It is a designed fragment-plus-stabilizer.

For structure, pathways, and open questions, see **[How Semax works](/how-it-works)**.

## What it is used as in Russia

In the Russian Federation, Semax is a **registered prescription nasal medicine** (nootropic / neuroprotective framing in domestic materials). Clinical and regulatory writing there has discussed it in contexts such as:

- cerebrovascular / post-stroke recovery settings 
- cognitive complaints linked to cerebrovascular disease or workload studies 
- optic-nerve disease protocols in ophthalmology 

It has also appeared on Russia’s **Vital and Essential Drugs** (ЖНВЛП) lists in some years (often cited around the 2011–2012 listing). That means Russian health-system policy treated it as a priority medicine for a period - not that every country agrees, and not that Western evidence standards have been met for the same claims.

**Plain takeaway:** In Russia it is a real pharmacy drug with a label and a prescription path. Elsewhere, the picture is different.

## Soft note on the “KGB peptide” and “Limitless” peptide search

People often land here after searching nicknames like **“KGB peptide,”** **“KGB brain spray,”** or **“Limitless” peptide**. Some people call Semax the **“Limitless” peptide** after the movie drug. That’s a nickname, not a scientific claim. Same class as **“KGB peptide”**: sticky search folklore, not chemistry or approval language. The documented origin story - Institute of Molecular Genetics (IMG), ACTH-fragment chemistry, Myasoedov & Ashmarin research lines - lives on the **[Origin](/origin)** page. We put the nickname on the door so curious readers can walk into a trustworthy room of primary sources, not vendor myth.
